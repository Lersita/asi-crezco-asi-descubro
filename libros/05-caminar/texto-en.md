## Experiment: How Much Force Does It Take? (pages 22–23)

**You need:** a shoebox, books or toys to fill it, a kitchen towel, and a ball.

1. Put the empty box on the floor. Push it with just one finger.
2. Fill it with books. Before you try, guess: will it move with one finger?
3. Try with one finger, then with one hand, then with both hands.
4. Put the full box on the kitchen towel. Pull the corner: does it come toward you?
5. **Challenge:** Push the ball gently, then hard. How many steps away does it go each time?

For the challenge, write down how many steps away the ball went each time.

**What we found out:** Pushing and pulling are forces: they make things move. Heavy things need more force: sometimes one finger isn't enough. And the harder we push the ball, the farther it goes.

## For grown-ups (page 24)

**The idea.** A force is a push or a pull. Moving something heavy takes more effort because it needs more force to get it going, and because it presses harder against the floor, which slows it down: that's the friction from Book 3. Walking is about forces too: with every step, the foot pushes the floor backward, and the floor pushes the body forward.

**The milestone.** Many babies take their first steps without holding on somewhere between 12 and 15 months; some earlier, some a little later. At first they walk with their legs wide apart and their arms up, and they often plop down on their bottom: it's part of learning. Every baby has their own pace; if you have any concerns, check with your pediatrician.

**Safety.** Walking takes them farther and faster: put gates on stairs, cover sharp furniture corners, and anchor to the wall anything that could tip over. At home, barefoot or in non-slip socks; outside, in flexible shoes. Pediatricians recommend not using baby walkers with wheels: sturdy, stable push toys are a better choice.

**Talk about it after reading:** How many steps is it from your bed to your bedroom door? And to the kitchen? What things in your home can you move by pushing? Which ones don't move even when you push hard? When you walk, who pushes whom: your foot pushes the floor, or the floor pushes you? (Both!)
