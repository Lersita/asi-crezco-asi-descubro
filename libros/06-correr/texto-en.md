## Experiment: Fast or Slow? (pages 22–23)

**You need:** a tray or a board, some books, a ball, an empty paper towel tube, and an empty plastic bottle with its cap on.

1. Rest one end of the tray on two books: that's a low ramp. Let go of the ball at the top, without pushing it.
2. Watch how far it goes before it stops.
3. Before you try, guess: if you add more books, will the ball go faster or slower?
4. Try it. Then let it roll onto a smooth floor and onto a rug: where does it stop sooner?
5. **Challenge:** Let go of the ball, the tube, and the bottle together. Which one comes in first? Which one second, and which one third?

**What we found out:** On the higher ramp, the ball reaches the bottom faster. Nothing stops all at once: it needs a moment and some space to come to a stop. On the rug, it stops sooner than on the smooth floor.

## For grown-ups (page 24)

**The idea.** Speed tells us how much distance we cover in a certain time: fast means a lot of distance in a short time. Going downhill, gravity makes a ball go faster and faster; going uphill, it slows the ball down. To stop, the body has to lose its speed, and that takes time and space: the feet and the friction with the ground slow it down little by little. That's why a running child can't stop all at once.

**The milestone.** Many children start running somewhere between 18 and 24 months: at first it's a trot with their legs wide apart and their arms out for balance, and they often plop down on their bottom. Stopping in time and turning while running come a little later. Every child has their own pace; if you have any concerns, check with your pediatrician.

**Safety.** Running takes them far in seconds: let them run in open spaces, with no steps or sharp furniture corners, away from the street, the pool, and the stairs, always with a grown-up close by. Comfortable shoes are best, and nothing in their mouth while they run.

**Talk about it after reading:** What things in your home go fast? And which ones go slowly? When you run, can you stop all at once? What happens to your feet? If three kids run a race, how many places are there? Who comes in third?
