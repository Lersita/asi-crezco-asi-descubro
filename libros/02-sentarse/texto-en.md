## Experiment: What Tips Over and What Doesn't? (pages 22–23)

Do this with a grown-up.

**You need:** a plastic cup, an empty toilet paper roll, a small box, a ball, an orange, and a thin book.

1. Do everything on the floor.
2. Set each thing on the floor and let go. Does it stay still or move? Write it down.
3. Now rest the book on top of each one. Does it hold the book, or does the book fall off?
4. Try the cup right side up and upside down. And the roll standing up and lying down. What changes?
5. Body challenge: sit on the floor with your legs together and your arms up. Then spread your legs and put your hands down. When do you feel steadier?

In each box, write or draw: yes or no.

**What we found out:** things with a wide, flat base stay still and can hold weight on top. Round things touch the floor at a tiny point: a little push moves them, and anything on top falls off. Sitting with your legs apart and your hands on the floor works the same way: your base gets wider, just like the box.

## For grown-ups (page 24)

**The idea.** An object stays balanced when its weight falls inside its base of support. The wider the base and the lower the weight, the harder it is to tip over. When a baby sits, they spread their legs and put their hands down in front: they build a wide base, like a tripod. With practice, their muscles learn to correct the wobble, and they no longer need their hands.

**The milestone.** Many babies start sitting with their hands propped in front somewhere between 6 and 8 months, and a little later they manage without help. Every baby has their own pace; if you have any concerns, check with your pediatrician.

**Safety.** At first, babies tip over often: practice on the floor, on a blanket, with cushions around and a grown-up close by. Never leave a baby sitting alone somewhere high, like a bed, a table, or a couch.

**Talk about it after reading**

- What things in your home are tall, and which are short?
- Why do chairs and tables have four legs?
- What's near you, and what's far from where you're sitting right now?
