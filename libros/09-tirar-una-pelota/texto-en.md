## Experiment: What Curve Does It Make? (pages 22–23)

**You need:** a rolled-up sock or a soft ball, a box or a basket, and masking tape or chalk.

1. With tape, mark a line on the floor to stand behind. Put the box three steps away.
2. Toss the sock gently and watch the path it makes in the air.
3. Before you go on, guess: if you throw harder, will it land closer or farther?
4. Throw gently, medium, and hard. Count how many steps away it lands each time.
5. **Challenge:** with tape, make a circle, a square, and a triangle on the floor. Which one can you land in?

**What we discovered:** Everything we throw makes a curve: it goes up and comes down, because gravity pulls it down from the moment it leaves our hand. The harder we throw, the longer the curve and the farther it goes.

## For grown-ups (page 24)

**The idea.** When something is thrown, it leaves the hand moving forward and upward. From that moment on, gravity slows it on the way up and speeds it up on the way down: that's why its path is a curve (physicists call it a parabola), not a straight line that drops at the end. With more force, the curve is longer. Rula is big and light, and the air slows it down quite a bit: a small, heavy ball thrown the same way would go farther.

**Shapes.** Spotting circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in everyday things is one of the first ideas of geometry. A ball is a sphere: from the front, it looks like a circle.

**The milestone.** Many children start throwing a ball overhand with one hand, and aiming, somewhere between 3 and 4 years old. At first they drop it or push it with both hands; good aim comes with practice. Every child has their own pace; if you have any concerns, check with your pediatrician.

**Safety.** Use soft balls, outdoors or in an open space, away from windows, glass, and the street, and never aim at anyone's face. Always with a grown-up close by.

**Things to talk about after reading:** What things are okay to throw? Which ones should never be thrown? What shape is the table, a plate, and your front door? If you toss a crumpled-up paper ball, what path does it make in the air?
